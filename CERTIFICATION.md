## 1. Certified Idle Machine Lab Graduate (grades 3–5)

### Requirements

A pupil earns the certificate when the teacher sees all of these. Teams are fine. Pupils may show what they know by
speaking, pointing or drawing, as in the assessments.

1. **Three unit projects.** The Week 6 (*Packet Relay*), Week 12 (*Share the Sum*) and Week 24 (*Ledger Keeper*, finished and commented) projects each score at
   least **2 (Developing)** on every row of the Scratch Project Rubric, and **3 (Secure)** on "Works".
2. **Capstone.** *The Idle Machine Network* scores at least **15 out of 24** on the Capstone Rubric ("meets" band),
   with at least **3** on "Network model" and on "Checking the work".
3. **Checks.** The Semester Check (Week 18) scores at least **5 out of 10** ("developing" band or higher). Teachers may
   retake or read it aloud.
4. **Safety.** The pupil can name two things they keep private (Unit 1 Check, question 5).

The unplugged capstone counts the same as the Scratch one if the school has no devices.

### How it is given

- The teacher prints the certificate (template to come from Omega Compute) and signs it in class.
- **Nothing is sent to Omega Compute about the pupil.** No names, photos, work, ages or counts by name. There is no
  online sign-up for pupils.
- The certificate itself shows no link, QR code, offer or product name. It is a "well done", nothing more.

### The Pewter offer for this course

Pewter's terms set a **minimum age of 13**. Children in grades 3–5 are younger, so:

- The offer is **never** made to a child, shown to a child, or put in pupil or family handouts.
- It may be offered to **the school or the teacher** (for example, for a staff or club account), or to **a parent or
  guardian's own account** if the school chooses to tell families about it in its own way.
- Claiming it needs no pupil data. The adult claims it for themselves.
- Amount, duration, eligibility and terms: **TBD by Omega Compute.**

## 2. Decentralized Computing Certificate (college)

### Requirements

Based on the course assessments in the syllabus (section 5):

1. **Labs.** Lab 0 passed, and at least **7 of Labs 1–8** with every required test passing in this repository's test
   suite, plus the short write-up for each.
2. **Lab 6 safety.** The smart-contract lab was done on a local chain only, as the lab requires.
3. **Problem sets.** All three submitted.
4. **Final project.** At least **70%** of the final project marks (proposal, code and evaluation, report,
   presentation).
5. **Ethics brief.** Submitted.

The certificate is separate from the course grade. A student can pass the course without seeking it, and the
instructor's grade never depends on it.

### How it is given

- The student asks for it. The instructor confirms the requirements (method TBD by Omega Compute; designed to share
  only what is needed, for example name, institution and a yes or no).
- Omega Compute issues the certificate.
- Self-learners who use the public labs without a course: route **TBD by Omega Compute**.

### The Pewter offer for this course

- Certified students may receive **free Pewter**, offered **directly to the student** by Omega Compute.
- Amount, duration, eligibility and terms: **TBD by Omega Compute.**
- Pewter's minimum age is 13. Its other terms still apply.
- The offer comes from Omega Compute, not from the instructor or institution. It is **optional**, it is not part of
  any grade, and no student is asked to sign up for Pewter to pass anything.
- Some institutions do not allow vendor offers to students. If yours does not, the instructor can leave this section
  out, and the certificate still stands.
